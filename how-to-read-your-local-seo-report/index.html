How to read your local SEO report: what home service owners should check each month

By Fix Scale Grow LLC · October 7, 2026

Every month a report lands in your inbox. It has charts, percentages, and a lot of green arrows. You skim it, close it, and go back to running jobs, because nothing in it tells you whether the phone rang more. That is a reporting problem, not a you problem.

A good local SEO report should help a roofer, plumber, or HVAC owner answer one plain question in a few minutes: is this work bringing in customers, and if not, where is it stuck? Here is what to look at, in the order we would look at it.

Start with calls, forms, and booked jobs

Rankings and traffic are signs that something might be working. Leads are the thing itself. Before anything else, find the number of phone calls, form submissions, and booked estimates that came from organic search and your Google Business Profile this month. Then compare it to the same month last year, not last month. Seasonal trades swing a lot. A furnace company will always look better in November than in May, and that tells you nothing about the marketing.

If your report does not show leads at all, ask for them. Call tracking and form tracking are standard tools, not extras. We wrote a longer piece on the three questions a marketing report should answer if you want a checklist to hand to whoever builds yours.

Check how your Google Business Profile is doing

For most home service businesses, the map results bring in more calls than the regular search listings below them. Your report should show how your profile performed: how many people saw it, how many called from it, and how many asked for directions or clicked through to your website. Google Business Profile has a Performance section with these numbers, so you can check them yourself in a couple of minutes.

Map rankings also change from neighborhood to neighborhood. You might sit at the top of the map near your shop and barely show up ten miles away. A grid-style map report, which checks your position at many points across your service area, tells you far more than a single ranking number. Our page on local SEO in Lincoln shows the kind of map coverage we look at for businesses close to home, and the same idea applies in any city.

Look at the actual searches, not just the totals

Google Search Console lists the searches that showed your website and how often people clicked. This is where you find out whether you are being found for the work you want. If most of your clicks come from searches for your own business name, that means people who already know you are finding you. That is good, but those are not new customers. The searches that matter most pair a service with a place, like water heater repair plus your town, or roof leak repair near me.

Scan the list for services you do not offer, too. If you are showing up for commercial work and you only do residential, your pages may be describing your business in a way that attracts the wrong calls.

Read traffic page by page

One total traffic number hides most of the story. Ask for visits and leads broken out by page. Your service pages and city pages should be doing the heavy lifting. If an old blog post brings in most of your visits but nobody who reads it ever calls, that traffic looks nice on a chart and does very little for the business.

Then check that the pages getting visits make it easy to act. A page with steady traffic and no calls usually has a problem you can spot in thirty seconds on your phone: no visible phone number, a slow load, or a form that asks for too much.

Treat reviews as part of the report

Reviews affect whether people find you and whether they call once they do. Each month, note how many new reviews came in, your average rating, and whether every review got a reply. A steady flow of recent reviews tends to mean more to a homeowner than a big batch from two years ago.

Questions worth asking every month

  • What changed this month, and what do you think caused it?
  • Which pages or services brought in actual leads?
  • Where are we weak on the map, and what is the plan for those areas?
  • What work was done this month, and what is planned for next month?
  • Is anything broken, like call tracking, a form, or the phone number on the site?

A good marketing partner will be glad to answer these. Vague answers are worth paying attention to, and so is a report that only ever shows good news. Every account has slow months, and an honest report shows them.

A fifteen minute monthly routine

  1. Find the leads from search and maps, and compare them to the same month last year.
  2. Open your Google Business Profile performance numbers and check calls and website clicks.
  3. Skim the top searches in Search Console for the services and towns you want.
  4. Look at your five busiest pages and make sure each one has a clear way to call or book.
  5. Count new reviews and make sure each one has a reply.
  6. Write down one question for your marketing partner and ask it.

That is the whole routine. You do not need to understand every chart. You need to know whether the work is turning into jobs, and where it is not.
